# n14-05163-triage: Late-order triage: cut driver fees on orders that can no longer be on time

- **Agent / branch / commit:** gemini · `exp/gemini-n14-05163-triage` · `HEAD`
- **Date:** 2026-11-14 05:55
- **Idea from:** `experiments/IDEAS.md` #15 ("Late-order triage: a car that cannot be on time is a cost to cut, not a rescue to pay for"), built on branch A (`origin/exp/gemini-n14-03324-supply-adaptive-fee-open-and-drop-rescue`)

## Why we tried it
Once `self.now > order["lastPickupAt"]` (or after an order's gig expires via `gig_expired`), the car can no longer be delivered on time regardless of how much we pay the driver, and the dealer trust penalty and on-time share hit are already unavoidable. However, `Desk.fees_for()` previously treated every order in `self.urgent` identically — posting and repricing already-lost orders on `Desk.steep()`, which opens at `1.80×` the typical fee and climbs every 30 minutes (including through the night) up to `3.00×`. Meanwhile, the late refund schedule only steps at +2 h (`10%`), +24 h (`25%`), and +2 business days (`50%`) of the order price, so paying `1.8×–3.0×` typical fee on an expired gig buys nothing that the refund table pays for (Fable point 9: each expired gig correlates with `€132` higher driver fees, ~`€1,150`/week).

Replacing `steep()` on lost orders (`self.lost`) with a calm daytime schedule that opens at `late_fee_open = 1.0×` typical fee and climbs in daytime half-hour steps to `late_fee_max = 1.3×` by the next refund-tier boundary minus the drive (`deadline + 24 h - drive`, then `deadline + 2 business days - drive`), escalating to `steep_max` (`3.0×`) only in the world's last 36 hours (when an undelivered car costs `2×` price), cuts the overpayment on lost cars while preserving on-time share. We also tested `late_repost` (immediately withdrawing and reposting an open unbooked gig without stop windows as soon as `self.now > order["lastPickupAt"]` in `tick()`, rather than waiting until `gig_expired`) and `late_fee_max = 1.1`.

## What changed
Merged `origin/exp/gemini-n14-03324-supply-adaptive-fee-open-and-drop-rescue` (branch A) and added three `Params` fields and telemetry counters in `src/spread_lab/policy.py` whose defaults (`late_fee_open=0.0`, `late_fee_max=1.3`, `late_repost=False`) reproduce branch A bit-for-bit (`Δmargin €+0.00 ± 0.00` on smoke):
- `late_fee_open: float = 0.0` & `late_fee_max: float = 1.3` — tracks `self.lost: set[str]` (orders whose gig expired in `gig_expired`, or unstaffed undelivered orders with `self.now > order["lastPickupAt"]`, rebuilt in `rejoin()`). When `late_fee_open > 0` and `order["id"] in self.lost`, `Desk.fees_for()` returns a daytime `rising()` schedule from `late_fee_open` to `late_fee_max` reaching the next refund-tier boundary minus remaining drive (`order["deadline"] + 24 * HOUR - drive`, then `add_business_days(order["deadline"], 2) - drive`), or climbing to `steep_max` in the last 36 hours before `ends_at` (`14,280`).
- `late_repost: bool = False` — when `True`, in `Desk.tick()`, if an undelivered unstaffed order has `self.now > order["lastPickupAt"]` and still has open unbooked gig(s), withdraws and reposts them without stop windows once per order (`self.late_reposted`).
- Telemetry counters in `self.stats`: `late_reposts`, `fee_lost_cents`, `fee_lost_n`, `fee_warned_cents`, `fee_warned_n` (from `gig_booked.feeCents`), and `late_le_2h`, `late_le_24h`, `late_more` (from `delivered.at - order["deadline"]`).

### Key Mechanism Findings
1. **A pays `€108.24` per lost booking (`€784.71/w` on smoke) — 40% more per booking than on warned orders that were actually saved (`€77.34/booking`):**
   - On smoke (`triage-noop`), A spent `€3,138.84` (`€784.71/w`) across 29 lost bookings (`€108.24` each) vs `€2,629.47` (`€657.37/w`) across 34 warned-and-saved bookings (`€77.34` each). Setting `late_fee_open = 1.0` (`late_fee_max = 1.3`) cut the average fee paid per lost booking on smoke by **25.6% (`€108.24 → €80.58`, saving `€220.63/w` directly on lost bookings and `€351.70/w` in total ledger `driver_fee`)**.
2. **On 16 dev seeds, `late_fee_open = 1.0` (`triage-open10-dev`) delivers `€+264.35 ± 63.80` (`t = 4.14`, 13/16 seeds better) over branch A and `€+1,851.96 ± 217.66` (`t = 8.51`, 16/16 seeds better) over Champion v2:**
   - Across the 16 dev worlds, `late_fee_open = 1.0` cuts `driver_fee` by **`€335.82/w`** (`-€18,636.52/w` vs `-€18,972.34/w` in A) and increases `revenue` by **`+€68.79/w`** (`+€29,997.30/w` vs `+€29,928.51/w`), far outweighing the `€63.52/w` increase in `late_refund` (`-€334.03/w` vs `-€270.51/w`) and `€81.40/w` in `undelivered_penalty`.
   - Crucially, keeping lost orders on a `1.0× → 1.3×` schedule prevents expired/lost gigs from sitting on the board at `2.8×–3.0×` overnight and cannibalizing morning drivers who would otherwise book active on-time gigs at `0.80×–0.90×`.
3. **Why `late_repost=False` beats `late_repost=True` (`€10,842.27` vs `€10,773.22`):**
   - Withdrawing and reposting an open gig immediately when `self.now > order["lastPickupAt"]` (`4.25/w` reposts on dev) posts a fresh windowless gig during peak afternoon hours, which competes with still-saveable `urgent` orders for scarce afternoon drivers (raising total late orders from `115` to `124`, particularly `<=2h` late orders from `27` to `37`, and cutting the `driver_fee` savings from `€335.82/w` to `€87.90/w`). Waiting until `gig_expired` (at the end of the order's window, typically 20:00) naturally defers lost cars until after the day's on-time deadline rush has cleared.

## Verdict
**adopt** — `late_fee_open=1.0` (`late_fee_max=1.3`, `late_repost=False`) on top of branch A (`runs/20261114-054842-triage-open10-dev`) reaches **`€10,842.27` mean margin (`Δmargin €+264.35 ± 63.80`, `t = 4.14`, 13/16 better vs branch A; `Δmargin €+1,851.96 ± 217.66`, `t = 8.51`, 16/16 better vs Champion v2)** with worst-seed on-time **`94.50%`** (`mean 97.41%`, **SAFE**).
