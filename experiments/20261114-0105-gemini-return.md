# return: direct return offers to drivers delivering within 30 km of an open gig

- **Agent / branch / commit:** gemini · `exp/gemini-n14-08-return`
- **Date:** 2026-11-14 01:05
- **Idea from:** `experiments/IDEAS.md` item #12 (a) ("Supply follows our cars: return offers")

## Why we tried it
A driver who delivers one of our cars far from home has to buy a public-transport ticket back unless they chain another gig starting near their delivery location. The app only notifies drivers of new gigs within 30 km of their *home* (`HANDBOOK.md`: *"the app does not notify a driver where your gigs have taken them"*), whereas we know from our bookings and `delivered` events exactly which driver is finishing where and when.

Idea #12 proposes a three-step sequence with a strict gate at step (a):
- **(a)** Implement `return_offer_mult` (default `0 = off`): when one of our booked drivers delivers within 30 km of an open gig's pickup in time to complete it, send them a direct offer for that gig at `return_offer_mult` times the typical fee (testing `0.8` and `1.0`). Measure the accepted share against the baseline deadline-warning offers (`3 of 218 = 1.38%`).
- **(b)** Only if (a) is accepted at least one time in five (`≥ 20%`), build `return_gate` to let thin-town requests pass the supply gate when a booked driver is heading there.

## What changed
Added `return_offer_mult: float = 0.0` to `Desk.Params` in `src/spread_lab/policy.py` and implemented return-offer dispatch and tracking in `Desk`:
- Tracked `gig_driver` (`gig_id -> driver_id`) across `gig_booked`, `offer_answered`, `gig_dropped`, `no_show`, and `rejoin()`.
- Tracked currently open offers per gig (`open_offers: dict[str, set[str]]`) alongside per-driver daily offers (`offers_sent[(driver_id, day)]`) to strictly enforce the `RULES.md` offer limits (at most 3 direct offers per driver per day, at most 3 open direct offers per gig, no offers 23:00–07:00, and matching `experienced` / `manualLicence` audience requirements).
- When a driver finishes delivering the final stop of a gig (`delivered`), `Desk.tick()` checks all currently open gigs whose first pickup is within 30 km straight-line distance (`geo.line_km(place_id, pickup_place) <= 30.0`) and reachable inside all stop windows, `lastPickupAt`, and order deadlines (`can_do_in_time`). Candidate gigs are ranked by same-day pickup availability and net travel time saved towards the driver's home (`minutes(place, pickup) + minutes(end_place, home) - minutes(place, home)`).
- Added counters to `desk.stats`: `return_offers` (sent), `return_offer_accepted`, `return_offer_declined`, `return_offer_expired`, and `return_late` (won orders booked via an accepted return offer that incurred a late refund or undelivered penalty).
- Also tested two diagnostic variants on `smoke` before the `dev` run:
  - `return-v2`: skipping drivers who already hold another active undelivered gig, requiring same-day pickup before the pickup site's closing hour, and matching newly posted gigs up to 60 minutes after delivery.
  - `return-v3-onway`: additionally triggering offers at `picked_up` (using estimated delivery time `at + road_minutes`) so drivers see the offer while en route.

Run command:
```sh
uv run lab eval -s return_offer_mult=1.0 --name return-dev
```

### Why Return Offers Are Read (~59%) Yet Almost Never Accepted (0.0%–1.07%)
Inspecting the event streams of the smoke worlds (`w3eeb7922475bcf950858`, `dev-04`) reveals three structural reasons why return offers fail to convert:
1. **Targeting delivering drivers fixes the *read* rate, not the *acceptance* rate:**
   In Fable's scouting of deadline-warning offers, 149 of 218 offers (`68.3%`) expired unread because drivers were not working that day. By targeting drivers who just delivered for us, **59.2% of our offers on dev (631 of 1,065) are read** (`620 declined` + `11 accepted`). However, 98.3% of drivers who read the offer at `1.0×` typical fee (and 100% at `0.8×`) decline it.
2. **Natural gig chaining happens hours *before* delivery:**
   In `dev-04`, out of 45 natural consecutive same-day gig pairs worked by the same driver, **all 45 (100%) were booked before the first gig was delivered** (`booked_at(g2) < delivered_at(g1)`), typically within minutes of `g2` being posted while the driver was planning their day or driving `g1`. By the time `delivered` fires, easy return gigs have already been booked on the app at the `0.85×` opening fee, and many delivering drivers either already hold their next gig (`25 of 84` offers in `dev-04`) or have exhausted their daily hours/gig limit (`36 of 84` expired). Even when we offered earlier at `picked_up` (`v3-onway`), acceptance only rose to `3 / 281 (1.07%)`.
3. **Adverse selection of open gigs on the board:**
   Because `min_supply=30` (`gate_radius_km=55`) declines thin-town pickups at quote time, drivers who finish long outbound drives in thin towns have no open gigs waiting there. Conversely, when drivers finish in dense towns (Capital, Hyvinkää, Tampere, Turku), the gigs still sitting unbooked on the board are predominantly outbound drives *away* from the driver's home (`41 of 47` declined offers in `dev-04`), where `0.8×–1.0×` of the typical fee does not cover the driver's added return ticket and time.

### Is Step (b) (`return_gate`) Worth Building?
**No — stop at (a).** Idea #12 requires an accepted share of at least **20% (1 in 5)** in step (a) before building `return_gate` in step (b). Our measured accepted share is **0.00% at `0.8×`** and **1.03% (`11 of 1,065`) at `1.0×`** on `dev` (compared with `3 of 218 = 1.38%` for deadline-warning offers). Relying on direct return offers to clear thin-town orders admitted by `return_gate` would leave ~99% of those orders unbooked by the delivering driver, exposing the desk to steep fee escalations, late refunds, and €300 undelivered penalties.

## Verdict
**reject** — `return_offer_mult=0.8` has **0.0% acceptance (0/328)** and `return_offer_mult=1.0` has **1.03% acceptance (11/1,065)** on dev with **Δmargin €-3.61 ± 60.66 (t = -0.06)**; stop at (a) and do not build (b).
