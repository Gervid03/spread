# n14-10-flu: Detect flu days from morning viewer drop and tighten same-day supply and win target

- **Agent / branch / commit:** gemini · exp/gemini-n14-10-flu · HEAD
- **Date:** 2026-11-14 01:12
- **Idea from:** IDEAS.md #14 ("Flu day")

## Why we tried it
At levels 7 and 8, every week has an unannounced **flu day** where 30% of the drivers who meant to work stay at home for the day (`HANDBOOK.md`, `RULES.md`). Drivers who work open the app in the morning and generate `gig_viewed` events. By comparing distinct viewers of our gigs per town by 10:00 against the same hour on earlier days, we aimed to detect a drop of `flu_drop` (0.25) or more and protect that day by raising `same_day_min_supply` by `flu_supply_mult` (2.0) and raising the price target (lowering the target win rate to `flu_target_win` = 0.25 or adding `flu_price_markup`).

## What changed
Added four fields to `Params` in `src/spread_lab/policy.py` (all defaulting to champion v2 behaviour):
- `flu_drop: float = 0.0` (0 = off; minimum fractional drop in distinct morning viewers per town by 10:00 against the mean of earlier days)
- `flu_supply_mult: float = 2.0` (multiplier on `same_day_min_supply` after 10:00 on a detected flu day)
- `flu_target_win: float = 0.25` (target win rate tracked by `Desk.learn` on a detected flu day, raising the price target)
- `flu_price_markup: float = 0.0` (optional direct price markup on a detected flu day)

Added `Desk.record_view` and `Desk.check_flu` (rebuilt in `Desk.rejoin`), plus `stats` counters: `flu_days`, `flu_day_{weekday}` (2=Wed, 3=Thu, 4=Fri), `won_flu`, `late_flu` (won on a flu day and ended late), `late_flu_deadline` (due on a flu day and ended late), and `dropped_flu_supply`.

Run with:
`uv run lab eval -s flu_drop=0.25 -s flu_supply_mult=2.0 -s flu_target_win=0.25 --name flu-dev`

### What the counters and reveals show
1. **Why `same_day_min_supply × 2.0` has almost no effect on v2 (`Δmargin = -€0.05 ± 0.71`):**
   Champion v2 already sets `same_day_min_supply = 100`. Across the 16 dev worlds, the capital pool (Helsinki, Espoo, Vantaa) always has 303–458 drivers within 30 km, which is well above `2.0 × 100 = 200`. Only a handful of mid-sized towns in a subset of worlds (Tampere/Lempäälä, Oulu, Turku/Raisio, Jyväskylä) have supply between 100 and 199. Across all 16 dev seeds, raising `same_day_min_supply` to 200 on detected flu days dropped `same_day` on only 27 options (`dropped_flu_supply: 27`), changing outcomes on just 3 of 16 seeds by pennies.
2. **Why raising the price target on detected flu days hurts margin (`Δmargin = -€174.36 ± 76.43`):**
   - In `flu-dev`, the detector fired 21 times across the 16 weeks (`flu_day_2`: 6 Wednesdays, `flu_day_3`: 8 Thursdays, `flu_day_4`: 7 Fridays), covering 706 won orders (`won_flu: 706`), of which 22 were late (`late_flu: 22`, or 3.1% — virtually identical to the 3.3% baseline late rate on non-flu days).
   - Morning viewer counts by 10:00 are heavily confounded by how many unbooked `next_day` gigs were carried overnight from the previous afternoon. After a price-war day (when the desk sits out and wins few orders), very few gigs are open on the board the next morning between 07:00 and 10:00 (e.g. `dev-06` Wednesday has 0 open morning gigs and only 9 viewers by 10:00 vs 140 on Tuesday), triggering a false flu alarm on the post-war recovery day.
   - Even on genuine flu days, 70% of working drivers are still active, and `next_day` / `within_3_days` orders won on a flu day are staffed the next day when the 1-day flu is over. Raising the global price target (`flu_target_win = 0.25`) sheds ~10.5 profitable slow orders per week (`won_mean` 217.3 vs 227.8) without improving on-time delivery (`on_time_min` unchanged at 94.36%, `on_time_mean` 96.67% vs 96.75%).

## Verdict
reject — Raising `same_day_min_supply` to 200 on detected flu days is a no-op (`€-0.05 ± 0.71`, worst on-time 94.4%) because v2 already gates `same_day` at 100 drivers, and raising the price target (`flu_target_win=0.25`) costs `€-174.36 ± 76.43` (`t = -2.28`) by shedding profitable `next_day` orders and firing after low-volume price-war days.
