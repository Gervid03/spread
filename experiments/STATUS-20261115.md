## Tonight's experiments (all dev-12, `experiments/orc/`; notes beside them)
Each arm is a fresh world of dev-12 played by a ~200-line desk; every arm has a control that wins the same cars and
sends each alone at 0.9× the typical fee. Saved: `~/.spread-lab/orc/dev-12/<script>-<arm>.json` (loops1 keeps every
event). Note: `orc.tape()` reads `~/.spread-lab/tas/<seed>/tape.json`, and loops1 reads `limits-all.jsonl` there; only
dev-12 is on this PC (copied from `spread-oracle-data/dev12.tgz`).

### Clearly working
- **Closed loops of 2–3 cars posted as ONE gig** (`loops1.py`, quotes at 0.92× each leg's bisected limit):
  - pairs: **+€1,597 vs control (€4,673 vs €3,076, +52%)**, costs −19%, on time 96.8% vs 99.4%; 116 of 119 booked
    (86 at 0.6×, 30 after the raise); pair cars late 9/238.
  - triangles: **+€643** (€3,990 vs €3,347), costs −21%; 49 of 54 booked (35 at 0.5×), late 6/162.
  - long pairs (pairs1, each car ≥ 90 min): costs −19%, ~€19 per paired car; short pairs −15%, ~€14 per car.
- **The recipe that made loops safe** (pairs1 run 1 → 2): open at 0.6× (0.5× for 3), **4 h to spare** on every
  deadline, **raise once to 0.9× after 2 h unbooked**. Without it (1 h spare, no raise) on time fell to 86–92%.
- **Quoting at ~0.92× the limit** wins 76–80% of the aimed legs (list price: ~55%).

### Clearly not working
- **4-car loops**: −€1,318 vs control; a missing leg breaks them (148 of 406 cars went alone), the ones that run are
  late 11% (ten hours, four deadlines). They book cheap (0.45×) when they run.
- **Fee search by releases** (the user's binary-search idea): −€15,180 (pairs1 bisect); release fees (25–50% of the
  fee) swamp the saving; the search ends at ~0.77×. The clean test (loops1 teach vs flat: Monday searched, then both
  arms flat 0.7× from Tuesday) shows **no learning**: after the search 32% of 0.7× gigs booked within 15 min vs 49%.
- **Offering the way back to the driver who booked one car** (pairs1 split): 0 of 15.
- **Ride home sold by direct offers** (loops1 homeonly: cars ending in a pool, in the app at 0.5× while offers at 0.75×
  go to drivers living near the delivery, 3 open, rounds of 10 min, 2 h): 3,027 offers, 77% unread, **2.5% accepted**;
  the waiting made cars late (87.5% vs 92%): **−€1,586**.

### Unsure
- **Chain home** (the user's idea, Sun 00:30): a driver living in A trains to B, drives B → C, then C' → A home; one
  gig, opened at 0.65× the two typical fees, raised to 0.9× after 2 h, plus aimed offers (at the app fee) to drivers
  living near A. 276 chains on dev-12 (552 cars, typical €73; Vantaa → Raisio/Turku/Oulu, Kuopio → Helsinki…).
  Arms `chain` / `solo6`: **+€954 vs control (€3,385 vs €2,431), costs −10%**, but on time 87.3% vs 88.0% (both
  unranked: these chains run long, into Oulu and Turku/Raisio). 130 chains posted, 110 booked (73 at 0.65×, 36 after
  the raise); chain cars late 34/260 (13%, vs ~4% for closed pairs); aimed offers: 9 of 1,934 accepted. So: it
  books cheaply, but the long open chains are late too often. Worth retrying with short chains and more spare.
- **Offers, aimed** (from loops1's events, 5,474 offers): read rate and acceptance by time since the driver's last view
  of one of our gigs: < 10 min: read 30%, accepted 2.0%; 10–60 min: 32% / 4.1%; 1–6 h: 28% / 4.4%; > 6 h: 19% / 0.8%;
  never viewed: 10% / 0.9%. Aiming quadruples acceptance from a small base. Offers cost nothing unless taken, so
  sending them always, aimed at drivers who viewed in the last hours, is a small free gain (the user's point).
- **Loops delay trust**: every loop arm won fewer of the same quotes than its control (294 vs 348, 285 vs 314).
  Holding cars for a partner delays deliveries, so dealer trust grows later.
- **Outer regions** (the user's strong intuition): on dev-12 "outer" is mostly **Hyvinkää** (161 requests out, 100 in,
  3 drivers, Vantaa 39 km away = just outside the 30 km notification radius of 359 capital drivers; 125/161 offer
  same_day; 44 go to the capital). Dealers do NOT pay more there: limit ÷ typical 1.51 (0–4 drivers near the pickup)
  vs 1.68 (100+). It is volume (~a quarter of requests) that only a cheap way to bring drivers can take. The final's
  world reshuffles which towns are thin. Literature: headhaul/backhaul (trucking), Bimpikis–Candogan–Saban 2019
  (with unbalanced flows, price by origin).
- **The oracle target** (~€23k on dev-12) is far: loops alone are worth ~€3–4k a week at this volume.
