# ladder: the twin ladder — let batches bracket the competitor

- **Agent / branch / commit:** gemini · `exp/gemini-n14-03-ladder` · `66554de`
- **Date:** 2026-11-14 00:46
- **Idea from:** `IDEAS.md` item #5 ("The twin ladder: let batches bracket the competitor")

## Why we tried it
About 45% of level-8 requests arrive in batches (144–152 `batchId`s per world, mostly 2–4 cars, plus same-dealer same-yard requests arriving within a few minutes). Quoting all cars of a batch at the same multiplier gives only a single binary signal ("above" or "below" the competitor), whereas spreading batch quotes across $	ext{estimate} 	imes \exp(\pm	ext{spread})$ allows a batch that both wins and loses to bracket the competitor's acceptance threshold in one step (between the dearest win and the cheapest loss). Furthermore, ordering open batch members by `referenceFeeCents` assigns the $-	ext{spread}$ discount rung to shorter/cheaper drives and the $+	ext{spread}$ markup rung to longer/more expensive drives.

## What changed
Added three fields to `Params` in `src/spread_lab/policy.py` (all defaulting to champion v2 behaviour):
- `ladder_spread: float = 0.0` (0 = off; open quoted members of a batch are spread evenly across $	ext{estimate} 	imes \exp(\pm	ext{ladder\_spread})$, ordered by `(referenceFeeCents, request_id)` so the cheapest/shortest car gets $-	ext{spread}$ and the longest/dearest car gets $+	ext{spread}$; earlier open batch members still before `respondBy` have their quotes updated in place or replaced).
- `ladder_lr: float = 0.0` (0 = off; when all members of a multi-request batch have resolved and the batch has both wins and losses, move `self.mult` toward the geometric midpoint $	ext{mid} = \sqrt{	ext{dearest\_win} 	imes 	ext{cheapest\_loss}}$ via $\exp(	ext{ladder\_lr} \cdot \log(	ext{mid} / 	ext{base\_mult}))$).
- `ladder_window_min: int = 5` (requests without `batchId` from the same dealer and pickup yard within this many minutes join the same open batch).
- Added `stats` counters: `batches_quoted` and `batches_bracketed`.

Run with: `uv run lab eval -s ladder_spread=0.05 -s ladder_lr=0.5 --name ladder-dev`

### What the counters and ledger breakdown show
1. **Bracketing rate:** On `dev` (`ladder-dev`), the desk ladder-quotes **79.1 batches/world** (`batches_quoted`) and **32.4 batches/world** (`batches_bracketed`, 41%) both win and lose, bracketing the competitor with `refused_quote = 0` on every world.
2. **Two discoveries in the mechanics:**
   - First, 60% of batches have size $k = 2$ (`[-s, +s]`), where the bracket midpoint $	ext{mid} = \sqrt{m_0 e^{-s} \cdot m_0 e^{+s}} = m_0$ equals the batch's starting estimate. Moving `self.mult` toward `log(mid / self.mult)` undoes the upward step that `Desk.learn()` takes on a 50% batch win rate toward the 35% target win rate (`price_target_win = 0.35`), pinning `self.mult` too low (the 4 unsorted smoke runs lost €99–€272). Measuring the bracket shift relative to `b["base_mult"]` (`log(mid / b["base_mult"])`) fixes this: symmetric 2-car brackets do not fight `price_target_win`, while asymmetric $k \ge 3$ brackets jump toward the sub-bracket midpoint.
   - Second, sorting batch members by `(referenceFeeCents, request_id)` so the shortest/cheapest car gets $-	ext{spread}$ and the longest/dearest car gets $+	ext{spread}$ turned smoke from −€102 to **+€400.58 ± 331.04** and improved dev driver fees by **€207.78/world** (−€17,079 vs −€17,287), reduced `deadline_close` warnings from **12.1 to 10.6/world**, reduced late refunds from −€376 to −€358/world, and raised mean on-time share by **+0.25%** (to 97.0%, min 94.2% SAFE) at virtually identical order volume (228.2 vs 227.8 won).
3. **Why net margin gain is small (+€45 ± 169) on v2:** Within a bracketed batch, the dealer accepts the $-	ext{spread}$ discount rung much more often than the $+	ext{spread}$ markup rung. That adverse-rung selection costs **€175.89/world** in gross revenue (€26,747 vs €26,922), which nearly cancels the €207.78/world driver-fee and €18.45/world late-refund savings once v2 already has `price_lr = 0.05`.

## Verdict
reject (keep disabled by default, `ladder_spread=0.0`) — SAFE and slightly positive on dev (+€45.41 ± 169.07, t=0.27, 8/8 better/worse, +0.25% on-time), as the €208/world fee + lateness savings from steering batch discounts to shorter drives are mostly offset by the −€176/world adverse-selection revenue cost on the lower ladder rung; worth re-testing alongside two-clock morning resets (`IDEAS.md` #3) where Monday/post-war brackets are far from equilibrium.
