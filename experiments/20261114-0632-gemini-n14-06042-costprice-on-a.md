## Why we tried it
The competitor's price includes a lane factor that follows how hard a lane is for drivers to staff, while the request's `referenceFeeCents` (typical fee) measures that lane's driver cost (median `0.90 × listPriceCents`). Idea #18 hypothesizes that quoting a flat multiplier on `listPriceCents` causes adverse selection—winning too many hard, high-cost lanes (`typical_fee / max_list >= 1.0`) and losing easy, low-cost lanes (`typical_fee / max_list < 0.8`)—and that tilting option prices by `clamp((referenceFeeCents / (fee_ratio_mid * max_list)) ** fee_weight, 1 / (1 + fee_tilt_max), 1 + fee_tilt_max)` before the margin floor, plus learning from winning floor quotes (`learn_from_floor=True`), will shift wins toward cheap, safe cars and reduce `driver_fee / revenue`.

## What changed
Merged `origin/exp/gemini-n14-03324-supply-adaptive-fee-open-and-drop-rescue` (base A) and added four `Params` fields and nine ratio-band counters in `src/spread_lab/policy.py` whose defaults (`fee_weight=0.0`, `fee_ratio_mid=0.9`, `fee_tilt_max=0.25`, `learn_from_floor=False`) reproduce A bit-for-bit (`Δmargin €+0.00 ± 0.00` on smoke):
- `fee_weight: float = 0.0`, `fee_ratio_mid: float = 0.9`, `fee_tilt_max: float = 0.25`: in `Desk.price()`, computes `ratio = request["referenceFeeCents"] / (p.fee_ratio_mid * max(o["listPriceCents"] for o in request["options"]))` and `tilt = clamp(ratio ** p.fee_weight, 1 / (1 + p.fee_tilt_max), 1 + p.fee_tilt_max)`, multiplying every option's pre-floor price by `tilt` when `p.fee_weight != 0`.
- `learn_from_floor: bool = False`: in `Desk.learn()`, when `True`, a quote with `self.decided[request_id] == "floor"` that wins (`won=True`) updates `self.mult` and `self.dealer_adj` upward just like a normal `"quote"` win, whereas a losing floor quote teaches nothing.
- Counters by third of `referenceFeeCents / max_list` (cut at `0.8` and `1.0` into `easy` `< 0.8`, `mid` `0.8–1.0`, `hard` `>= 1.0`): non-declined quotes (`q_easy`, `q_mid`, `q_hard`), won orders (`w_easy`, `w_mid`, `w_hard`), and late orders (`late_easy`, `late_mid`, `late_hard`) from `late_refund` and `undelivered_penalty` ledger lines.
- Added `experiments/scripts/analyze_costprice.py` to summarize paired comparisons against A and Champion v2, win/late rates by ratio third, and `driver_fee / revenue`.

## Results (level 8, paired against A and against Champion v2)

### 1. Does A win a larger share of the hard third than of the easy third?
**No — the opposite holds on A.** On `costprice-noop` (smoke, 4 worlds), A's win rates and late rates across the three thirds of `referenceFeeCents / max_list` are:
- **Easy (`< 0.8`):** **46.8% win rate** (`325 / 694` quotes), **4.00% late rate** (`13` late orders)
- **Mid (`0.8 – 1.0`):** **40.3% win rate** (`647 / 1,604` quotes), **2.16% late rate** (`14` late orders)
- **Hard (`>= 1.0`):** **31.1% win rate** (`179 / 576` quotes), **2.79% late rate** (`5` late orders)

Why A already prices out the hard third without `fee_weight`:
1. **The margin floor (`floor = 1.25 * referenceFeeCents / 0.92 = 1.3587 * referenceFeeCents`) already acts as a `w = 1.0` cost price on high-ratio requests:** `701` of `2,874` non-declined quotes on smoke (`24.4%`) sit on the floor, almost all in the `hard` and upper-`mid` thirds, lowering the `hard` win rate to `31.1%` vs `46.8%` on `easy`.
2. **After A's supply, drive-length, and slack gates filter out unstaffable requests, the remaining `hard` third is neither late nor unprofitable:** on smoke, `hard` wins have a lower late share (`2.79%`) than `easy` wins (`4.00%`) and carry larger per-order euro margins because `easy` requests (`referenceFeeCents / max_list < 0.8`) are predominantly short/cheap or `same_day`-heavy requests where `max_list` is inflated by the `same_day` option price (`next_day ≈ 0.93 × same_day`).

What went wrong:
1. **`fee_weight > 0` reduces `driver_fee / revenue` as intended (`63.39% → 62.77%` on dev at `w=0.25`, and `61.00% → 59.26%` on smoke at `w=0.50`), but loses €342/w on dev because it swaps large-ticket orders for small-ticket orders and unmasks floor losses into the tracker:**
   - On dev, `fee_weight=0.25` keeps total order volume almost unchanged (`271.8/w` vs `273.8/w` on A), shifting wins from `hard` (`24.1%`) into `easy` (`49.4%`). However, revenue falls by **-€1,516.56/w** (`€29,928.51 → €28,411.95`) while driver fees fall by only **-€1,138.90/w** (`€18,972.34 → €17,833.44`). Even though percentage cost `driver_fee / revenue` improves by `-0.62` percentage points, a higher percentage margin on smaller `easy` requests yields fewer total euros of gross margin.
   - Furthermore, multiplying `hard` requests up before the floor lifts `106` quotes on smoke (`701 → 595` at `w=0.25`, `701 → 464` at `w=0.75`) above `floor`, reclassifying them from `decided = "floor"` (which does not teach on loss) to `decided = "quote"`. Because those tilted-up `hard` quotes lose ~76–86% of the time, `Desk.learn()` treats their losses as normal tracker losses and pushes `self.mult` downward across all requests, underpricing the `easy` and `mid` thirds.
2. **One-sided `learn_from_floor=True` creates an upward ratchet on `self.mult` that starves volume:**
   - Updating `self.mult` upward by `+(1 - price_target_win) * price_lr` when a floor quote wins while ignoring floor quote losses adds positive drift to `self.mult` and `self.dealer_adj` every time a floor quote is accepted (~31% of floor quotes). On dev (`costprice-w025-lff-dev`), this ratchets prices up, cutting `driver_fee / revenue` to `60.27%` (`€15,994 / €26,538`) but losing `-27.0` orders/week (`271.8/w → 244.8/w`) and `-€1,874/w` in revenue (`Δmargin €-373.43 ± 165.67` vs A).

## Verdict
**reject** — Both `fee_weight=0.25` (`Δmargin €-342.23 ± 154.44`, `t = -2.22`, 4/12 vs A; `+€1,245.38 ± 185.95` vs Champion v2) and `fee_weight=0.25, learn_from_floor=True` (`Δmargin €-373.43 ± 165.67`, `t = -2.25`, 6/10 vs A; `+€1,214.19 ± 174.04` vs Champion v2) reduce `driver_fee / revenue` (`63.39% → 62.77%` and `60.27%`) as predicted, but lose total euro margin on dev because A's margin floor already reverses the adverse selection (`easy` win rate `46.8%` vs `hard` win rate `31.1%`). Keep `fee_weight=0.0` and `learn_from_floor=False` (A's defaults).
