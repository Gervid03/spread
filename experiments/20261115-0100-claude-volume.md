# Volume: how could we win Juhus's +46% orders? (Sun 15 Nov ~00:00–02:00)

His question after the trust/offers round: Juhus makes 1.58× our desk on the same board worlds, and it is mostly
volume (2,419 vs 1,659 orders, +46%; €43.6 vs €40.1 an order, +9%). How can we make that happen? Plus: leftover cars
chained back-to-back, same-day into the capital, and his rule for the final version.

## His rule for the final version (Sun ~00:30)
- Things that matter little but are clearly the right thing to do go into the final desk (his examples: sending direct
  offers, counting trust in). Things that are sketchy and at noise level get skipped.
- Measured against it: offers at the app fee are free (nothing paid unless accepted) but did nothing (3 of ~9,600);
  pricing trust explicitly *lost* €314 ± 126 on 16 worlds. So by the data, trust pricing is not "clearly right" in the
  form tested; offers are harmless.

## Where v7 loses volume (16 dev worlds, champion-equivalent run)
- Per world: ~1,000 requests; **~277 won**, ~276 declined outright (`quote_decline`), the rest lost on price.
  Margin €43.7 an order (revenue €110, costs €66).
- Decline reasons per world (per option): Friday same-day 56, same-day supply 50, slack 30, long drive 20, thin long
  next-day 18, next-day long 15, backlog 5; plus the supply gate (`min_supply` 22 drivers within 55 km).

- **Leftover chains**: chains that end in a pool booked at their opening 0.65× (14 booked); chains ending elsewhere
  booked only after the raise, at 0.90× (48). Only 5 of 62 went to a driver living at the end. So the chain works only
  when it ends where drivers live: the cheapness is the ride home, as the handbook says ("a gig that ends near a
  driver's home is cheap for that driver").
- **Same-day into pools** works in the oracle (Hyvinkää → Vantaa/Helsinki/Espoo, Kouvola → Helsinki, Lempäälä → Vantaa:
  68 of 69 booked, 98.5% on time) but not as a live rule: the live desk quotes more of them and wins few, so the
  oracle's gain is its price knowledge, not the lane.
- **The oracle week fails on capacity**: winning ~450 cars at the limits floods the drivers. With flat fees ~20% of cars
  are late; with a ladder they arrive (86–90%) but cost what they earn. Past ~280–300 cars a world, a marginal car costs
  about its price at our fees. So Juhus's +46% volume at +9% an order means they staff cars far cheaper than we do.

## Shapes are capacity
Cars per driver-day (dev-12, delivered): pairs 1.93 vs the same cars alone 1.35 (96 drivers vs 170); triangles 1.83 vs
1.31. Drivers also chain our separate gigs on their own (54 driver-days with two cars in the solo control).

## A mechanism we haven't used: drivers learn our raises
Handbook, *Drivers learn your fees*: each driver keeps a running mean of (booked fee ÷ first viewed fee) and of the minutes
to booking, over orders they viewed; **"a system that always raises its fees teaches drivers to wait."** v7 opens at
0.85× and rises every half hour to 1.60× at the deadline warning, so its drivers expect raises and hold out. A desk that
posts the right fee and never raises teaches them to book at once. Tested: flat schedules (`fee_warn = fee_open`;
the steep schedule after the warning stays), 16 dev worlds:
| flat at | Δ margin | won | on-time min | fees a world | late refunds |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v7 (0.85 → 1.60) | — | 277 | 94.7% | €17,913 | €330 |
| 0.9 | −€1,517 ± 142 (worse on 16) | 276 | 86.5% (2 unranked) | €18,093 | €853 |
| 1.0 | −€1,515 ± 157 (worse on 16) | 283 | 93.2% | €19,299 | €525 |
| 1.1 | −€2,551 ± 165 (worse on 16) | 284 | 92.2% | €20,963 | €406 |
- Clear no: the rising schedule finds cheaper drivers than any flat fee, waiting included. Within one week the
  learning costs less than the price discrimination over time gains.

- Taking cars from towns with 8–21 drivers within 55 km is worth ~€300–600 a world, i.e. ~+25 orders, but the busiest
  world then runs ~2.5 points later. The guard can't see it coming: its projection stays high until the late cars land.
- Not promoted. It is the right direction for his volume question (more cars from thinner towns), but it needs a
  better lateness guard first.

## What it says
1. Volume is capped by drivers, not prices. Winning more on price (live or oracle) only adds cars that cost what they
   earn; past ~280–300 cars a world our marginal car is worth ~0.
2. The only levers that added margin with volume: shapes that end where drivers live (pairs, triangles, chains into a
   pool), and quoting slightly thinner towns (min_supply 8: +€283–625, with a lateness tail).
3. v7's rising fee schedule beats flat fees by €1.5k+; drivers' learning of our raises shows no cost within a week.
4. Juhus's volume at a higher margin per order means they staff cars much cheaper than we do; our measurements say the
   way is shapes (1.9 cars per driver-day vs 1.35), which v7 does only for ~13% of its cars.
