### The one constraint that decides what counts: the final is live
The final (Sun 08:00) and the test finals are played **once, in real time**, by `lab final` on Verda.
- **Real time.** The 20:00 test final and the final play the whole week, Monday to the end, in about 34 wall minutes:
  about 7 game minutes per wall second. A request's answer window (15 game minutes) is **about 2 wall seconds**, and
  the desk handles every event as it streams (`play_live` in `src/spread_lab/runner.py`). Practice waits for the desk;
  the live world doesn't. So a rule must decide in milliseconds, and `tick()` must stay cheap, because it runs after
  every event.
- **No forks, no what-ifs, no replay, no agent in the loop.** Co-play, `whatif`, `probe` and `scripts/tas.py` are for
  *finding* a rule. What ships is deterministic code in the Desk (`src/spread_lab/policy.py`, or
  `src/spread_lab/policies/`) that decides from what the desk can see at that moment. A table computed beforehand from
  practice worlds is fine, if it holds on worlds it wasn't fitted on (holdout, night-01..32).
- **A late join is possible.** The 14:00 test final started at Thursday 00:00 with Monday–Wednesday already played as
  lost (614 requests, no orders, no trust); v6 made €2,448 there. `-s live_from_min=4320` makes the desk sit out until
  Thursday 00:00 in practice, so you can measure that case (dev-12 gives €2,556 with v6). The final plays from Monday,
  so this is a side check, not the target.
- **The rate is shared.** The server allows the team about 1,000 requests a minute in total, for all ~12 agents. Eight
  worlds in parallel are not faster than eight one after another. So: **one eval at a time per agent**, no polling loops
  on `/events` or `/score`, and screen on a few worlds before you spend sixteen.
