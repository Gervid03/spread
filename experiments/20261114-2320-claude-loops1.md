# loops1: 2–4 car loops vs pairs, won near the limits; and the "teach" test (dev-12, Sat 14 Nov ~23:00)

Script: [`orc/loops1.py`](orc/loops1.py) (every event of every world saved: `~/.spread-lab/orc/dev-12/loops1-<arm>.json`).
Follows [pairs1](20261114-2230-claude-pairs1.md). Every loop leg quoted at 0.92× its bisected limit
(`limits-all.jsonl`), not at the list price. Loops opened flat at 0.6× (2 cars), 0.5× (3), 0.45× (4) the summed typical
fees, raised once to 0.9× after 2 h; 4 h spare on every deadline.

## The question his way: one driver doing a day of 3–4 cars, or many drivers each taking one car home?
| arm | targeted cars | won | on time | margin | costs | as one gig |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| loops (up to 4) | 406 (94 squares, 6 triangles, 6 pairs) | 308 | 91.2% | €1,810 | €18,320 | 42 loops posted; 35 squares, 26 booked (17 at 0.45×, 9 after the raise); square cars late 15 / 140 |
| its control (all alone) | 406 | 321 | 96.9% | €3,128 | €17,632 | — |
| **pairs** | 356 (178 pairs) | 285 | 96.8% | **€4,673** | €12,181 | 119 pairs posted, 116 booked (86 at 0.6×, 30 after the raise); pair cars late 9 / 238 |
| its control (all alone) | 356 | 314 | 99.4% | €3,076 | €15,113 | — |

- **Many drivers, one car home each, wins clearly.** Pairs: +€1,597 over their control (+52% margin, −19% costs), on
  time 96.8% vs 99.4%.
- **Squares lose**: a square needs all four legs won, so most break (148 cars went alone), and the ones that run are
  late 11% of the time (one driver, a ten-hour day, four deadlines). When a square books, it's cheap (0.45×).
- Quoting at 0.92× the limit wins 76–80% of the targeted legs (pairs1 at the list price: ~55%). Not all: the limits
  were measured on v5's week, whose trust and lateness differ from these desks'.

- About two thirds of single cars book within 15 minutes; a fifth only after 2 h (the next-day cars, booked when
  drivers know their day). So pairs1's bisect rule "unbooked for an hour = too cheap" was wrong for ~20% of cars:
  part of its loss was the parameter.

## teach vs flat: does a Monday of release-searches make drivers book lower later?
Both arms post every car alone; from Tuesday every car flat at 0.7×. Teach searches Monday's cars by releases.
| arm | won | on time | margin | release fees | Tue+ cars at 0.7×: booked / within 15 min |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| teach | 269 | 87.4% | −€3,290 | €4,904 (72 Monday cars) | 171 of 201 / 54 (32%) |
| flat | 302 | 87.1% | €1,390 | — | 263 of 305 / 129 (49%) |
- **No teaching effect**: after the Monday search, drivers booked 0.7× gigs no faster (slower, if anything). The idea is
  rejected on its own terms, not just on its release fees.
- 0.7× flat is too low for single cars (both arms ~87% on time).
