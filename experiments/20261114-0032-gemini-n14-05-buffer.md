# n14-05-buffer: Give drivers an earlier deadline than the dealer's

- **Agent / branch / commit:** gemini · `exp/gemini-n14-05-buffer`
- **Date:** 2026-11-14 00:32
- **Idea from:** IDEAS.md #9 ("Give drivers an earlier deadline than the dealer's") + Research R3 ("How late is late")

## Why we tried it
Drivers plan their day to reach each stop inside its window (`RULES.md`: "they leave later rather than wait at a closed site"), while real drive durations vary around map road minutes. A driver who plans to arrive at 19:55 for a 20:00 deadline and experiences +10 minutes of variance arrives at 20:05 — missing both the deadline and the dealer's 20:00 closing hour, which turns a few minutes of road delay into an overnight wait until the yard reopens the next morning.

  - Driver arrival lateness (`delivered` event timestamp − effective deadline): min **5m**, median **105m**, mean **144.4m**, max **396m** (`[5, 9, 25, 46, 58, 83, 95, 105, 105, 114, 116, 173, 184, 287, 319, 335, 396]`).

Three of these (`9m`, `25m`, `58m`) arrived just after the 20:00 yard closing and waited overnight, recording **780m, 720m, and 660m** of ledger lateness (25% refund).

Thus, the target class for Idea #9 — orders that had a driver and missed the dealer's deadline by minutes — is **5 of 41 late orders (~1.25 orders per week)**, all arriving within `58` minutes (`<= 45 + 0.1 * drive`) of the deadline.

## What changed
Added two parameters to `Params` in `src/spread_lab/policy.py` (both defaulting to `0` so champion behaviour is unchanged):
- `delivery_buffer_min: int = 0` — minutes subtracted from the delivery stop's window end when posting a dealer delivery.
- `buffer_per_drive: float = 0.0` — additional buffer minutes per road minute of drive (`int(delivery_buffer_min + buffer_per_drive * drive + 0.5)`), clamped so the delivery window never ends before the earliest feasible arrival (`max(now, pickup_start) + drive + plates + charging`). When a gig expires (`gig_expired`), `repost()` strips custom stop windows via `without_windows(action["stops"], float("inf"))`, reverting to the order's own window.
- Added `stats` counters: `posts_buffered`, `late` (alongside existing `gig_expired`).

Run with: `uv run lab eval -s delivery_buffer_min=30 -s buffer_per_drive=0 --name n14-05-buffer-30-0-dev`

### Why the trade-off is slightly negative on margin
1. **Small target pool:** As R3 showed, only ~1.25 orders per week fail because a booked driver misses the 20:00 deadline by minutes (`5–58m`); most held-at-warning late orders come from unparsed note deadlines or trade-in slots. On dev, `(30, 0)` removes 0.38 late orders per week (`7.38 → 7.00`, improving mean on-time share by `+0.19%` to `96.94%`).
2. **Earlier booking cutoff vs late expiry:** Under `RULES.md`, a driver can book a gig only if `now + drive <= delivery_window.to`, while `gig_expired` does not fire until `delivery_window.to + 1`. Narrowing `delivery_window.to` by 30–60 minutes on all ~230 posts per week reduces the pool of drivers who fit early in the fee ramp and makes an unbooked gig unbookable `buffer` minutes *before* `lastPickupAt` (while waiting for `delivery_window.to` to expire). This pushes +1.0 more orders per week into `deadline_close` (`12.1 → 13.1`) and +0.9 into `gig_expired` (`7.6 → 8.5`), increasing mean driver fees by €273.67/week.

## Verdict
reject — `(delivery_buffer_min=30, buffer_per_drive=0)` improves dev mean on-time share by `+0.19%` (`7.38 → 7.00` late orders/week, SAFE at `93.8%` min), but tightening all ~230 delivery windows increases `deadline_close` (+1.0/week), `gig_expired` (+0.9/week), and driver fees (+€274/week), yielding `Δmargin €-91.41 ± 156.85` (`t = -0.58`).
