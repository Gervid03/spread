# pairs1: chosen closed pairs, posted as one gig (dev-12, Sat 14 Nov ~22:15)

Script: [`orc/pairs1.py`](orc/pairs1.py). Results: `~/.spread-lab/orc/dev-12/pairs1*-<arm>.json`.

## The question
The Spread II lesson's claim: about two thirds of a typical fee is the driver's trip home, so a car that closes a loop
should cost far less than a car alone (fork probes: closed loops 0.31–0.43× the summed typical fees, one car
0.78–0.84×). Our oracle picked cars first and toured them afterwards; its tours booked 63 of 102. Choose the pairs
first: do they book as one gig, at what fee, and do they arrive on time?

## Set-up
- From dev-12's tape: 880 one-car dealer requests with a slow option; a maximum matching of closed pairs (each end
  within 30 km of the other car's other end, under 700 road minutes, both deadlines met with SPARE to spare, arriving
  within a day of each other): **180 pairs, 360 cars**.
- Quote only those, at the list price of the slowest option; decline the rest.
- A won car waits for its partner. Both won: one 2-car gig, flat at MULT × the two typical fees. Partner lost, or the
  car's last pickup under 6 h away: alone at 0.9×. Rescue: deadline warning → 1.4×; expiry / failed visit → alone at
  1.4×.
- Arms on the same world: pairs at 0.45×, pairs at 0.6×, and a control posting every car alone at 0.9×.

## Run 1 (SPARE 1 h, no raise)
| arm | won | on time | pairs posted | booked as pairs (first try) | pair cars late (booked pairs) | late overall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.45× | 194 | 85.6% | 50 | 37 (74%), at 0.51× incl. rescue reprices | 3 / 76 | 28: 22 of them cars of the 13 pairs that expired, reposted alone |
| 0.6× | 204 | 92.2% | 52 | 47 (90%), at 0.60× | 4 / 96 | 16: 9 from expired pairs |
| solo 0.9× | 212 | 99.1% | — | — | — | 2 |

- **A booked pair is cheap and on time**: ~0.5–0.6× the two typical fees, about 4% late.
- **An unbooked pair is the risk**: its cars go out alone too late (the rescue at the deadline warning is 3 h before
  the last pickup: too late to find a driver). That is where all the extra lateness comes from.
- Only ~55% of the targeted requests were won at the list price, so only ~50 of the 180 pairs formed: winning both
  halves needs a price near each dealer's limit (the oracle knows it; a live desk must buy the closer).
- Margins here mean little: the list price is far below the limits.

(Costs = revenue − margin from the score: fees, refunds and fines. A multi-car gig's fee line names no order, so a
per-order sum misses it.)

| run 1 | margin | costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.45× | €298 | €11,382 |
| 0.6× | €1,981 | €10,238 |
| solo | €973 | €11,829 |

## Run 2 (SPARE 4 h, one raise to 0.9× after 2 h unbooked)
| arm | won | on time | margin | costs | pairs posted | booked as pairs | raised once | pair cars late |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.45× | 207 | 96.1% | €2,415 | €10,133 | 62 | 59, at 0.66× on average | 31 | 3 / 120 |
| **0.6×** | 209 | **98.1%** | **€2,599** | **€10,058** | 65 | 63, at 0.69× on average | 22 | 3 / 128 |
| solo 0.9× | 213 | 98.6% | €977 | €11,885 | — | — | — | — |

- **The fix works.** With 4 h to spare and one raise after 2 h, pairs keep on-time at the control's level (98.1% vs
  98.6%), and the 0.6× arm costs **€1,827 less** than the control on about the same cars: **−15% of all costs**,
  about **€14 per paired car** (128 cars in pairs).
- A third of the pairs at 0.6× (22 of 65) needed the raise; at 0.45× half did. So the price at which a short closed
  pair books within 2 h is ~0.6×; the average booked fee with the raise is ~0.69×.
- **Correction to the lesson's envelope.** The lesson assumed ~€33 saved per paired car. The pairs that can be chosen
  are mostly short capital lanes (Vantaa, Hyvinkää, Espoo, Helsinki pickups; typical fee ~€51 a car), where the
  saving is ~0.2–0.3× the typical fee: **~€11–14 a car**. At +200 paired cars that is ~+€2.5k on dev-12, not +€6–7k.
  The long loops (0.31–0.43× in the fork probes) save more per car but are rarer: they are where to look next.

## What it says
1. Choosing pairs first works mechanically: they book, cheaply, and on time, if they leave time to spare and are
   raised once when they don't book.
2. The money in pairs is modest on short lanes. The big savings need long loops (capital ↔ Tampere / Turku / Lahti
   and back), and winning both halves needs prices near the limits.
3. Next questions, in order: (a) the same with long loops only (road ≥ 3 h); (b) quote the pair legs near their
   limits (`limits-all.jsonl`) instead of at the list price, to see how many pairs form and what the week earns.
