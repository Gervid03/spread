## A story for two minutes (a suggestion; the numbers are dev-12 unless marked)
1. **The problem**: a car-transport dispatcher in a driver shortage. Price each dealer request (a competitor bids too),
   then find a driver for every car you win, on time. Margin = price − driver fee − late refunds.
2. **The noise**: we measured every dealer's exact limit by binary search on copies of the world. About half of
   the price variance is predictable (option, towns, day, dealer, trust, the competitor's moves); the rest (sd 0.17)
   is noise no one can see.
   Everything predictable adds ~10%; a perfect oracle would add +64% on top. So the race is won on the cost side.
3. **Drivers are the bottleneck, and about 2/3 of a typical fee is the driver's trip home.** A car that ends near a
   driver's home is cheap for them.
4. **Shapes**: a closed pair posted as one gig books at ~0.6× the typical fees: **+€1.6k (+52%) over the same cars
   sent alone**, costs −19%; triangles −21%; pairs move 1.93 cars per driver-day vs 1.35 alone. Chains that end where
   drivers live book at 0.65×; chains ending elsewhere only at 0.9×. Drivers find these gigs far beyond the 30 km
   notification ring (41 of 106 chains were booked by a driver living at the chain's end).
5. **What didn't work, and why** (short, honest): fee search by releases (−€15k: release fees), offers at the app fee
   (3 of ~9,600 accepted: an offer works only as a hidden raise), return offers the minute a driver delivers (3 of
   174), flat fees (−€1.5k: the rising schedule is a reverse auction; drivers learning to wait costs less), buying trust
   with price cuts.
6. **Real life**: selling a loop at every node; letting drivers see the return leg; pricing by origin in thin regions
   (Bimpikis–Candogan–Saban 2019). The game rules forbid some of these.
7. **The desk that plays the final**: see `experiments/PROMOTIONS.md` for what was promoted overnight (if anything
   beyond v7), and the final's result if it is in by then.
